- **Starting a run no longer costs a pass over every possible descriptor number** (R6.75).
  - Every run that loads a model registers itself and starts a small detached helper that clears dead records. Before it started, that helper closed one descriptor number after another up to the process limit. Where the limit is 1,048,576 (a common setting on macOS shells and in containers) that is a million calls with a handful of descriptors open: 0.14 s of CPU for each helper on an Apple M1.
  - The helper is not waited for, so nothing slowed the caller down to match. A script that makes many short runs started helpers faster than the machine retired them: 175 were alive at once on an 8-core laptop, with a load average over 200.
  - On macOS the helper is now started in one call that hands over no descriptor at all. On Linux it is one `close_range` call as before; where the kernel has none or a sandbox refuses it, the helper closes what it inherited as its first act, listing only the descriptors that are open. No loop over the limit remains.
  - Measured on the Apple M1, limit 1,048,576: the helper has used 1.8 ms of CPU when it starts, where it had used 143.6 ms. 150 short runs one after another take 9.1 s where they took 11.9 s.
  - `test-instances` holds it: with the limit raised, the helper must have used less than half of what one close per number costs, must hold none of its caller's descriptors, and must lead its own session.
