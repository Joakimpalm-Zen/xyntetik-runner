- **`--train-gpu` steps are about 1.35x faster on an RTX 5070 host, and the adapter is byte-identical to before** (R8.24.1).
  - Measured on Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M with the ToolUse training record's data (rank 8 on every projection, alpha 16, ctx 128, 20 steps, 8 threads): 1.34 s a step against 1.81 for main d9b54174, interleaved runs, the median of steps 2 to 20. The adapter's sha256 is the one main writes, run after run, and a CPU run's adapter is unchanged too.
  - Device mode's step was mostly host work and copies, not kernels: about 3.1 GB a step crossed the bus from pageable memory, every product's output was copied once more on the host, and three loops over the window's FFN ran on one thread. Four changes, each alone the same bits:
    - the FFN's three elementwise loops of the backward run on the thread pool by whole positions, in device mode only (0.22 s of a 1.8 s step);
    - a gradient that starts at zero is cleared on the device instead of sent (`gpu_train_mvt0`), and the products land straight in their destination (0.10 s);
    - products of one input send it once, with one synchronization (`gpu_train_mm_parts`, 0.03 s);
    - the forward's batch scratch is registered with the driver for the run and the backward's four FFN planes come from pinned blocks, so their copies run at pinned speed (0.09 s). Memory the driver will not pin stays pageable, with the same bits.
  - Tried and dropped: the head's per-row softmax on the pool measured no gain (about 0.02 s of the step).
  - Not changed here: the CPU still runs attention, the norms, rope and the adapter's own products, and about 2.6 GB a step still crosses the bus; keeping the step on the device needs new kernels.
