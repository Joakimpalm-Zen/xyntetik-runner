- **A bounded number is never led past its bound** (R2.19).
  - Under `{"type": "number", "maximum": 1}` the validator admitted `1e+36`. After it no byte was legal, so a constrained request stalled with every token masked, and a forced close wrote `1e+36`, out of bounds. A real output showed it: `2.74e+57` under a maximum of 10, in the JSONSchemaBench corpus. Now a number's prefix is admitted only while some completion of it lies inside the bounds, so the exponent digit that would leave none (the `3` of `1e+3`) is refused where it is written. That holds for both bounds, both signs and exclusive bounds, and also for a mantissa no scale can bring inside a narrow interval: under `[0.5, 0.6]` a number cannot start with `1`. A forced close writes a completion that is checked to lie inside.
  - A number is spelled in at most 95 characters, the most the validator keeps, so a completion must fit in what is left. The shortcut for wide intervals did not look at that room: under `maximum: 1` a run of nines was admitted to the 95th byte, where the exponent that brings it inside no longer fit and every byte was refused. Now 91 nines are the most it admits (`e-91` completes them), and a point or an exponent mark is refused at the 95th byte, where its digit has no room.
  - Checked against a brute-force search over completions on 15,206 next-byte decisions in 8 schemas: 0 admitted without a completion, 0 refused with one.
