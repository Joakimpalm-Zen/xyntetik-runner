- **On the CPU a prompt's bits no longer depend on the batch size** (R3.60).
  - A prompt is fed in chunks of `-b` rows. A chunk's short tail, and a MoE expert that a chunk gave fewer than 8 rows, used to take another arithmetic than the rest of the prompt, so the logits after a prompt moved in their last bits with `-b`, with where the chunks fell, and with how much of the prompt the prefix cache had kept. On a CPU-only run every product of a prompt's feed now takes the same batch route at every width; decode is unchanged. A prompt repeated from the prefix cache now gets the cold feed's bits too.
  - Measured, small batch first: on gpt-oss-120b MXFP4 (CPU, 26 threads) the prompt read 1.146x as fast at `-b 64` and 1.084x at `-b 512`. On an Apple M1 the dense CPU prompt cost read 0.994x at worst (SmolLM2-1.7B Q4_K_M, 11 rounds). Identity held at `-b 64` against `-b 512` on gpt-oss-120b (two prompts) and on a dense file (seven lengths), each with a live control.
  - Not moved: the Apple GPU and CUDA keep their routes, their CPU-resident layers and host experts included. A version that changed the Apple GPU too read 0.952x on a short prompt, under its 0.97x line. Echo (`prompt_logprobs`), option scoring and `--score` keep the decode step's numbers. CPU prompt logits differ from earlier releases in their last bits; no greedy token changed in any read.
