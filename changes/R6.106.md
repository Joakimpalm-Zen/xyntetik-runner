- **A portable-math build made with gcc 13 gets its vector exp loop back: the attention softmax runs about 3.9x faster there, and no output changes** (R6.106).
  - The softmax's exp loop in such a build (`-DRUNNER_PORTABLE_MATH` on the default flags; a T3 build includes the portable exp too) was scalar on gcc 13 at every x86-64 level we checked (x86-64-v3, x86-64-v4, native), and `make test-pmath-vec` failed there. `p_expf` (src/pmath.h) ended in a `?:` that picks the quieted NaN or the result. gcc 13 moved the whole non-NaN computation into that `?:`'s arm and then did not if-convert the branch. gcc 15 vectorized the same source.
  - The select is now a mask over the bits: both values are computed and the mask picks one. Every float gives the same bits as before. `make test-pmath-expf-all` and `make test-pmath-expf-all-default` check all 2^32 inputs, and 0 differ.
  - On one x86-64 CPU at -march=native (4096-wide rows, best of 9, pinned to one core, two runs), gcc 13's exp loop took 3.31 to 3.43 ns an element before and 0.52 to 0.54 after, and the whole softmax 3.75 to 3.86 and 0.97 to 0.98. With gcc 15 the exp loop took 0.52 to 0.64 and the softmax 0.97 to 1.18, before and after alike (the spread is noise).
