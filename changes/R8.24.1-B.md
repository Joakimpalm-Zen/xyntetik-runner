- **`--train-gpu` runs a step's layers on the device whole: on an RTX 5070 host a step is 2.67x faster than Runner 1.20.0's (d9b54174) and 2.04x faster than main before the change (cc7af33b); a default build's adapter bytes change once** (R8.24.1, list B).
  - Limits: a default build's adapter is not a T3 build's yet. The head's loss and adjoint and the optimizer stay on the host, under `-ffast-math` and the C library's `expf` in a default build, so its adapter differs from both main's default bytes and the T3 build's; both are within the tolerance below. The documented tolerance against a CPU run (1e-4 over 4 steps) is shown on the test fixtures. On a real model a training run is a trajectory, and any two arithmetic variants drift apart at the 1e-3 level within a few steps; on the recipe below, each step's loss against a T3 CPU run's: step 1 (the forward alone) 1.9e-4, steps 2 to 5 8.4e-4, 2.4e-3, 9.5e-4, 2.8e-4, inside the spread of main's own four builds ([docs/lora.md](docs/lora.md#training-on-the-gpu---train-gpu) has the table). The step time and the result below were read on one host, an RTX 5070 beside a Ryzen 7 7700X, on Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M only.
  - On a dense model in the resident step's scope (Qwen3, Llama and their kin: every layer with its own Q, K, V and O, a gated SiLU FFN, RMS norms without post norms, no biases on the attention's projections, adapters of rank 512 or less on the seven projections, a window of at most 12288 rows), every taped forward round and the backward's recompute and adjoints run on the device: the products, the norms, rope, the attention, the FFN's activation and the adapters at all seven projections, on a tape and K/V rows kept on the device. The head's adjoint goes up once a step and the adapters' gradients come back once, so the optimizer, the gradient norms and checkpoints are as before. Outside the scope, or when the step's planes do not fit beside the weights, the run says once on stderr why and takes the same kernels piece by piece; `RUNNER_TRAIN_RESIDENT=0` keeps that path and `RUNNER_TRAIN_DEVICE_STEP=0` the host's FFN, for comparison. A resident step that fails on the device fails the run with an error. The record's `products.resident_steps` counts the steps that ran resident, and the device's planes are freed with the training context.
  - The new kernels are host-chain twins (`kernels_train.cuh`): each element computed as a strict build's host computes it (`make T3=1`: the host's order of operations, nothing contracted, the portable exp of `src/pmath.h`). A T3 build's `--train-gpu` adapter is therefore the one it was before, byte for byte (Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M, 5 steps), and on the test fixtures in the resident scope (Llama's shape without Q/K norms, and with them) a T3 build's resident step gives the per-piece path's gradients by bits. A default build's adapter changes once, because its host had used the C library's `expf` and `-ffast-math` in the parts that moved. The CPU trainer's bytes do not change.
  - Measured on that host (Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M, the ToolUse training record's data, rank 8 on every projection, ctx 128, 20 steps, 8 threads; the median of steps 2 to 20, interleaved runs on 2026-11-18, at a3589d19, whose tree is 6d3a65d9's on this branch): 0.67 s a step, against 1.79 s for d9b54174, 1.37 s for main cc7af33b and 1.06 s on the per-piece path.
  - The trained adapter does what main's does: on the record's full recipe (316 steps, read at a3589d19, the tree of 6d3a65d9) it makes the exact tool call on all 29 of the record's held-out prompts at every checkpoint from step 20 to 316, as main cc7af33b's does (the record's scorer, main's serving binary for both; the base alone 0.690), with main's loss curve within rounding.
